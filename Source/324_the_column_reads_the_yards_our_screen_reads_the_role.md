## 1. THE ROW THAT MATTERS, AND I HAD IT BACKWARDS

Boone ranks **Caleb Douglas #6 overall** and writes of the same receiver room: *"His teammate,
Malik Washington, is also a potential flex option."* A dart-throw, in his own list. **I drafted a
recommendation to move claim 3 from Washington to Douglas on that basis, and then checked.**

| | snap % | targets | half-PPR | our week-1 screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Malik Washington** | **98%** | **8** | 4.8 | **3 of 3** (targets 8 · 98% snaps · share 30) |
| Caleb Douglas | 91% | 7 | 11.9 | 2 of 3 (91% snaps · share 26) |

**Boone is reading the YARDS and our screen reads the ROLE.** Douglas had the better afternoon: 94
yards on 7 targets against Washington's 4.8 half-PPR on 8. Washington had more snaps, more targets
and the larger share, which is what doc 308's screen is built on and what doc 235 measured as the
thing that predicts. **On this wire, in this league, Washington is the better version of the same
bet, and the column's ranking would have cost the swap.**

**AND NEITHER IS A COMPOSITE HIT.** Scored on §4.30's own population (2025 season rates):
Washington **1 of 3** (round 6, 4.98 yards per target, 3.82 targets a game); Vele **2 of 3**. The
composite's table is 5.0% at one signal and 7.1% at two against 39.4% at three. **Both sit in the
flat part of the curve. There is no 3-of-3 receiver anywhere in this column.**
**And Douglas cannot be scored at all** — he is a rookie, and the composite's population requires a
prior season. Applying it to him would have been §0.5(a2) in a new place, and I nearly did.

## 2. THE BLACK CASE IS THINNER THAN THE COLUMN

Boone: *"the 49ers split their backfield snaps 36-28... CMC poured in 88 scrimmage yards on 15
touches, Black wasn't far behind with 70 yards on 16 chances."*

| | snap % | targets | carries | touches | half-PPR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Christian McCaffrey | 55% | **8** | 10 | **18** | 11.3 |
| Kaelon Black | 43% | **1** | 14 | 15 | 7.5 |

**The column does not mention targets once, and in half-PPR the receiving split is the whole
story.** Eight to one is not a committee, it is a two-down runner behind a back who owns the
passing down. The seat is still real — the page prices that job at **302**, the largest on our wire
— and doc 240's rule stands: a bench back earns his spot by the job he would inherit and the
fragility of the man ahead. **But as a week-2 flex, which is how the column sells him, one target
is the number to hold onto.** He is also the most contested name on the board at 15 touches, which
is doc 314's top band: 2.27 filers, 56% contested.

## 3. THE POPULATION KILLS HALF THE LIST (§0.6)

His roster percentages are **Yahoo**, and his FAB column is a **$100 cap** — we have no FAAB at
all, which §4.31 already records as the reason the 4for4 FAAB split does not apply here either.

**Free in our league:** Douglas (15.6%), Washington (7.7%), Vele (13.1%), Black (15.5%),
Schultz (19.6%).
**NOT free here:** KC Concepcion, Denzel Boston, Jonah Coleman, Kenyon Sadiq, Deebo Samuel Sr. —

**One measured disagreement worth recording even though it is unactionable.** Boone names
**Boston** (NFL round 2, pick 39) as the Browns rookie to hold and mentions Concepcion mainly for
his fumble. Our own numbers give **Concepcion** more targets (5 to 4) and more touches (8 to 4);
Boston's edge is one touchdown, and §4.5 measured red-zone touchdown rate at r=+0.02, noise.
**§4.28 is the strongest rookie-receiver finding in the project: first-round rookies displaced at
60.0% against 3.3% for rounds 2 to 3, Fisher p=0.000001.** Concepcion went 24th overall; Boston
went 39th. **Our method points at the other man.** Both are rostered here, so nothing follows for
this week — but it is a clean dated call the takes corpus can score in January, and Boone is show
number 7 on that corpus's own list.

## 5. THE RULE

**An outside column is a source, and a source gets the same check as a hunch.** Three files and
four minutes turned a recommendation I had already drafted into its opposite. The column was not
wrong about football; it was ranked on a different quantity than the one this league's decision
turns on, and nothing but reading our own rows would have shown that.
