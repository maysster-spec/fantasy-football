SHOW: Fantasy Life Show (Matthew Berry's Fantasy Life)

THE LAST ATTEMPT RETURNED 17 LINKS AND NOTHING BEFORE 2023. Do not repeat it. Here is why it
failed and what to do instead.

WHY IT FAILED: you searched Apple Podcasts. Apple's pages are not scrapeable and carry no usable
per-episode links for my purpose. This show's episodes live on YouTube and on fantasylife.com.

DO NOT USE podcasts.apple.com AT ALL. Do not use Spotify, iHeart, Podchaser, Goodpods, Deezer,
Podcast Republic, Metacast or Musixmatch either. I cannot use any of them.

WHERE TO LOOK, and only here:
  https://www.youtube.com/@MBFantasyLifeShow/videos
  https://www.youtube.com/@MBFantasyLifeShow/search?query=waiver
  https://www.fantasylife.com

ANSWER THIS FIRST, IN ONE LINE, BEFORE ANY LINKS: what is the publish date of the OLDEST video on
that YouTube channel? If the show did not exist in 2021 or 2022, say so plainly and return nothing
for those seasons. Do not pad a season from a different show or a different feed.

THE METHOD, AND IT IS AN ENUMERATION, NOT A SEARCH. Do not ask "find the waiver episodes". Work
through a grid, one cell at a time, and report every cell:

  For each season the show existed (2023, 2024, 2025 at least), and for each NFL week 1 to 14:
    - The waiver wire episode for that week. A daily show does this on the Tuesday or Wednesday.
    - NFL week 1 begins the Thursday after Labor Day. Week N's waiver episode therefore publishes
      in the days right after week N's Sunday. Use the channel's date filter and go week by week.
    - If a week has more than one candidate, take the ONE whose title most clearly names waivers,
      adds, pickups, claims or the wire.
    - ONE per week. Fourteen per season. No more.

  Then, separately, every PRESEASON sleepers, breakouts, busts, post-hype or draft-targets episode,
  usually July or August. There should only be a handful per season and I want all of them.

EVERYTHING ELSE THIS SHOW PUBLISHES IS OUT OF SCOPE: news reactions, DFS, best ball, dynasty,
mailbags, Sunday previews, Monday recaps, betting, interviews, and anything that is not a weekly
waiver episode or a preseason sleepers episode.

THE LINK RULE: one link per episode, youtube.com/watch?v=... only, or the show's own page on
fantasylife.com if the episode is genuinely not on YouTube. Never a channel, a playlist, a search
results page, an archive index, a tag page or a third-party recap.

RETURN FOUR PARTS, IN THIS ORDER, AND NOTHING ELSE.

PART 0. The oldest video's publish date, one line, as demanded above.

PART 1. THE COVERAGE GRID. One row per season, fourteen columns for weeks 1 to 14, plus a final
row per season for the preseason episodes. Put the episode title in the cell, or the word MISSING
if you checked that week and found nothing. I read this grid to see what you skipped, so write
MISSING rather than leaving a cell blank or dropping a season.

PART 2. THE LINKS. One block per season, under a heading that is just the year. Inside each block,
one URL per line and nothing else on the line: no bullets, no numbers, no titles, no commentary,
no markdown headings, no hash marks.

PART 3. One line per season: how many of the fourteen weeks you found, and how many you checked
and could not find.

Do not curate, rank, filter or recommend. If an episode looks routine, that is the reason to
include it: routine episodes are the denominator and skipping them is the one thing that ruins
this.

Send me what it returns. I add the links to the year files and this page, and the new show goes into the coverage count.

Step 2 · one notebook per season

Four to build. Fifteen minutes each, most of it waiting. Never mix two seasons in one notebook: the season is the join key to what actually happened and it is the one thing that cannot be undone afterwards.

  1. 1

    New notebook, named for the year

    Call it exactly Takes 2022, Takes 2023 and so on. The name is how you will tell them apart in six weeks.

  2. 2

    Add sources, Websites, one paste

    Copy that year's block below and paste the whole thing into the one box. Notebook accepts many URLs separated by new lines. You do not paste per show any more.

    Some will fail to import. That is normal and it is information: tell me which ones and I take them out of the list rather than leaving you to retry them.

  3. 3

    Configure Chat, once, per notebook

    Custom style, paste the standing rules from step 3 below, response length Longer. This is the single biggest saving: the rules then apply to every answer in that notebook and you never paste them again.

  4. 4

    Run the batches

    Paste the extraction prompt once. After that, the short command in step 5 is the whole of your job until the year is done.

  5. 5

    Save every batch as its own file

    Into 10_Gemni\Takes\, named 2022_batch1.csv, 2022_batch2.csv and so on. One file per batch, never one growing file. If a batch comes back wrong you throw away one file instead of untangling a merged one.

Standing rulespaste into Configure Chat, Custom style
You are converting fantasy football podcast transcripts into rows of a dataset that will be
scored against what actually happened. These rules apply to every answer in this notebook.

1. You are not summarising and you are not selecting the interesting parts. Completeness
   matters more than insight. A player an analyst mentioned and was WRONG about is the most
   valuable row in this file, because without those rows there is no hit rate to compute.
2. Never add a player an analyst did not name. Never quote a number they did not say.
3. If you cannot tell who spoke, write UNATTRIBUTED rather than guessing.
4. Speech to text mangles player names. When a token is probably a garbled name, correct it
   and say so. If you cannot identify it, write UNKNOWN rather than a plausible player. A
   wrong name is worse than a missing one, because a wrong name joins to the wrong outcome.
5. Do not rank, order or editorialise rows. Emit them in the order they were discussed.
6. Every row names the source it came from, spelled exactly as the source panel spells it.
7. Output preference, in this order: if you have code execution, run it and export a CSV.
   If you do not, output a strict Markdown table and nothing else, and save it as a Note.

   assumes full PPR and four-point passing touchdowns. Say so whenever you use one.
