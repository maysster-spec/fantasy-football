The weekly wire

Read from ESPN Friday 2 October 07:30 (DAILY). Next kickoff on file: Sunday 4 October 09:30 (week 4, IND at WSH); a claimed man locks with his game. The routine, the stash list and the pool, ranked on our value.

The routine

  1. Nothing to run. The scheduled tasks rebuild this page; by hand it is . f.bat, never py wire.py.
  2. Wednesday night: put the claims in, before 3am Thursday. Claims execute Thursday morning; a claim placed Sunday runs in the same batch and gives up four days of news.
  3. Order the claims: the man you would most hate to miss goes first. Only your first winning claim comes at your real priority.
  4. A drop on every claim. A waiver claim with no drop fails on the roster limit one time in six; the same drop on several claims is a hedge (the first winner takes it); two men you want both need two drops. Never a man in the IR slot.
  5. If the button says Add, add now. A free agent costs no priority. If it says Claim, he processes Thursday.
  6. Thursday and Sunday: set the lineup. Kicker on matchup; defense held through a soft run (the run is below).

Two lanes, kept apart. The hole: somebody is hurt or on bye and you need a body Sunday (quarterback and tight end are easy, receiver needs discipline, running back is close to hopeless). The gem: a man who cannot start for you today and takes over a real job the week the starter goes down. The gem is claimed before the injury.

The rules that decide a claim

Stream the one-slot positions; build the bench at the many-slot ones. Your added quarterbacks worked about a third of the time, your added receivers about one in eight. A second quarterback or tight end plays on a bye or an injury and nowhere else: claim one for a week you can see, start him, let him go.

The wire cannot fix a running-back hole. Four of every five backs you add never give you a startable stretch; the bench is built with backs.

Buy the job, not the name. The flag says a backfield is unresolved and what the job pays if he wins it; it has no opinion on who wins it.

The wire thins but does not dry up. The best free men are about as good in November as in September.

Coming up — plan these now, not in the week

Buy the bye cover two or three weeks ahead, when nobody is bidding. Defense: take the schedule (the matchup is worth more than the gap between the units). Quarterback: take the better man, matchup only to split two you rate the same (about a point a game against a four-point spread). Backs and tight ends: the opponent’s points allowed to the position this season is a tiebreak within a point, never more. Receivers: matchup is worth zero week to week, except the playoff box below.
Where the line is posted, the number beside a name is the line. A defense is ranked on its opponent’s implied total, lowest first; a quarterback on his own team’s total, highest first, and when it disagrees with the soft-matchup rule take the total. Why the gem is claimed early

You lose five of every six players another team also wants (10 of 63 over two seasons) and land 56% of the ones nobody else claims. Priority resets each week to inverse standings, so the better your team, the later you pick. This week you are 11 of 12, and the 10 teams who pick before you are WGTS, POT, FLEM, BC, DUCK, ???, Boo, ART, Tets, TURD.

Your long list works: a median of ten claims a week against the league's two, and at least one lands in 97% of the weeks you try. Point it at the man nobody is bidding on yet.

Being first wins you the player, not the right one: alone you land 56% against 16% when somebody else bids, but a man the league later came for was startable 22% of the time against 24% for one nobody chased.
