The columns that need explaining

columnwhat it is
VBDSeason points above a replacement-level starter at his position. The pure "how good is he" number, comparable across positions.
adds nowPoints this player adds to your STARTING LINEUP if you take him right now. Lower than VBD when you already have that position filled.
if I waitWhat the best player at the same position is expected to add if you skip him and take him at your NEXT turn instead.
wait cost (Δ)adds now minus if I wait. A tempo number, not the ranking. Big Δ = his position falls off a cliff before your next turn. Small Δ = that position can wait. The list is NOT sorted by this — see cost vs #1.
cost vs #1This is what orders the list, and it is the only bar on the page — a longer bar means further behind, and a FULL bar means 20 points or more behind, at every pick. Every row is scored by simulating your whole remaining draft after taking him; this column is that score minus row 1’s. free = row 1, the recommendation. tie = within 1.5 pts, which is closer than the engine can measure — take whichever you prefer, it costs nothing. Grey = under 6 pts, cheap. Red = it is not cheap.
still there?Chance he is still on the board at your NEXT turn. 80% = he probably lasts, so you can take someone else first. 10% = now or never. Blank on row 1 — you are taking him now, so it does not apply. goes first = this row is a tie with the recommendation AND at least 20 points less likely to survive to your next turn. It is not an argument that the engine is wrong. The engine already simulates who will be gone, so a tie is a tie after counting that — the mark tells you which of the tied rows you can only get on this turn. Use it when you have something the board does not: a news line on the card, an analyst call, a badge. Deliberately rare — it needs a tie, a 20-point gap, and a player the market actually prices.
the GONE columnThe last 14 players taken, newest at the top. NOW is the pick that just happened, then -1, -2 and so on counting back. The top three are brightened. 14 is not a round number. From slot 8 exactly 8 or 14 picks happen between your turns, so this column always holds everything taken since you last picked — and on the long waits (32, 56, 80, 104, 128, 152) it is exactly that window, ending at -13. It is not trying to replace ESPN’s full pick list.
the dashed TIER BREAK lineA real cliff inside the twelve rows on screen — the number is how many VBD points drop across it. The threshold adapts to the spread actually showing, so a flat board draws none. There are always exactly three slots for these lines; when fewer cliffs exist the rest are invisible spacers, which is what stops the page changing height every refresh.
RUN: 4 RB of the last 6A position run, next to the recommendation. It only fires at 4 or more of the last 6 — three of six is the ordinary state of a 12-team draft, and a warning that is always on is not a warning. Only the position actually running is named.

The marks beside a name

At most two judgement marks per row — one caution on the left, one edge on the right. That cap is deliberate. Click any row to open the full card: injury history, the bull and bear cases, the backfield, and any dated news.

markwherewhat it means
