# 250 — He is right: a Week-1 claim costs nothing in this league, and my caution imported a rule from a different game

**2026-09-09.** Matt: *"so why NOT take a chance week one? That was my point."*

**He is right, the answer is in his own settings file, and I had it in front of me one message
earlier when he told me the caution was budget-shaped.**

---

## 1. THE SETTINGS, QUOTED (§3: an uploaded source is ground truth)

`2026_League_Settings.txt`, lines 121–126:

```
Acquisition and Waiver Rules:
- Player Acquisition System: Waivers
- Season Acquisition Limit: No Limit
- Waiver Period: 2 Days
- Waiver Order: Reset Each Week to Inverse Order of Standings
```

**Three separate costs that exist in other leagues do not exist in this one.**
- **No budget.** Not FAAB. A claim spends no money.
- **No season cap.** "No Limit" — a claim spends none of a finite allowance.
- **AND NO PRIORITY COST, WHICH IS THE ONE I GOT WRONG.** The order **resets each week to inverse
  standings**. It is a function of his RECORD, not of what he claimed last week. **Winning a claim
  does not push him back; losing one does not move him up.**

**So the marginal cost of a Week-1 claim is exactly one thing: the roster spot — the player he
drops.** Nothing else.

---

## 2. WHAT I DID WRONG, PRECISELY

Doc 249 §4 said the FAB caution *"does not vanish, it changes currency — our cost is WAIVER
PRIORITY."* **That sentence is half right and the half that is wrong is the operative half.** I
translated budget into priority and then reasoned about priority **as if it were consumable**, which
is the FAB model wearing a different hat. It is not consumable here. It resets.

**And Matt had already told me.** *"Some of the things Ray may provide toward caution may only apply
to those leagues on a FAB budget."* **He supplied the correction in the same message that supplied
the transcript, and I applied it to the noun and not to the logic.** `ERROR_PATTERNS` §0.5(a2) in a
new shape: the object was right, the cost model behind it was borrowed from the wrong league.

---

## 3. WHAT THE ODDS ACTUALLY MEAN ONCE THE CLAIM IS FREE

Doc 224 measured that Matt wins **51 of 91 uncontested claims (56%)** and **10 of 63 contested ones
(16%)**. **I have been reading that as a reason for restraint. It is not — a lost claim costs
nothing.** Under a free claim the 16% is not a price, it is a probability, and the correct posture
inverts:

> **CLAIM OFTEN AND CLAIM EARLY. Failure is free. The ONLY scarce thing is the roster spot.**

And the rates Ray quoted stop being cautionary at all once the claim is free. **37–39% of Week-1
top-24 receivers stay top-24 (doc 249, ours and his). Against a 24-slot field that is enormous.**
His 28% for a surprise star is a warning about **what to PAY**, and in this league the answer to
"what do I pay" is "a drop." **If the man being dropped is worth less than a 28% shot at a starter,
the claim is correct on its face.**

---

## 4. THE STANDING RULE, RESTATED

**OLD (docs 224/225, as I have been applying it): the gem must be claimed BEFORE the job opens,
because five of six contested claims are lost.**
**CORRECTED: the gem should STILL be claimed before the job opens — but not because a contested
claim is expensive. It is because a contested claim is unlikely to LAND, and an uncontested one is
free.** So:
1. **Submit the speculative claim anyway.** It costs a drop only if it wins.
2. **Order the claim list so the pre-emptive names sit ABOVE the Sunday-night obvious ones** — the
   obvious one will be lost to somebody with a better record and the pre-emptive one will not.
3. **The binding constraint is the 15-man roster, and it is a DROP decision, not a claim decision.**
   §6/doc 240 already priced his weakest holding: **Spears at roughly −5 against the best
   alternative use of the spot.** That is the seat a Week-1 swing comes out of.
4. **The three IR slots are separate and free** (§6). Any IR-eligible designation on the roster
   opens a claim slot at no cost at all.
