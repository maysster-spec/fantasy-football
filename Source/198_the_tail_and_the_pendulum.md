## 3. THE PENDULUM — Matt asks whether the standing-critic instruction went too far

**My honest read: the critic mandate is not what is costing us. The reflex to convert every idea
into the nearest testable proxy is.**

Three times tonight he had to restate an idea I had already "tested":
- "boom or bust" → I measured season-to-season variance. He meant week-to-week reliability.
- "signals together" → I read it as a bigger regression. He meant *find the subgroup*.
- "vacated targets" → I measured the mean. He meant the tail.

**In all three he was right, and in none of them was the problem that I was too critical.** The
problem is that testing the literal version is *easy* and interpreting the intended version is
*hard*, so I default to the easy one and call the result an answer. §0.5(a) already names this —
*"reading through to intent is the job"* — and I have been failing the second half of that rule
while executing the first half enthusiastically.

**So: do not soften the critic instruction. Add one step in front of it.** Before testing anything
he proposes: **state what would have to be true for him to be right, and test THAT.** Tonight's
best result came from doing exactly that — the vacated-target tail exists because he insisted the
mean was hiding it.
