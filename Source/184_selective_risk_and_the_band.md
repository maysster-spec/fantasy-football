## 1. THE FINDING: "risk from round 9, never before" is answering a different question

§4.13's rule reads *"risk from round 9 (picks 104, 113, 128, 137). Never before."* That was measured
on a **global upside tilt across the whole draft**, which costs **$22–41, p=0.003**. It is a correct
finding about a *strategy*.

**It is not a finding about a single swing, and §4.13b's own table says the opposite:**

| ADP band | ratio "breakout" | **startable at all** |
|---|---|---|
| 25–48 | 2.1% | 75.0% |
| **49–84** | **8.5%** | **57.7%** |
| 85–120 | 7.2% | 37.7% |
| 121–180 | 17.0% | **20.5%** |

**A swing at ADP 49–84 produces a startable player 57.7% of the time. The round-9+ dart produces
one 20.5% of the time — nearly three times worse.** The late band beats its *price* more often
because the price is near zero; it delivers a *player* far less often.

**So "never before round 9" should not be read as "never take a swing before round 9."** It means
do not tilt the *whole board* toward upside. **One selective swing in the 49–84 band — Matt's picks
56, 65 and 80 — is the highest-value risk on the board, and the directive has been steering away
from it.** `[TESTED — this is §4.13b's own table, re-read against a question it was not asked]`

**§6 carries the corollary already and it also points the same way:** auditions concentrate in
rounds 5–8, because that is where the keeper rate is 15.1% against 8.3% after. Two independent
findings, same window.

## 2. Matt's three added Burden factors: right size, wrong lever, legitimate use

*Caleb Williams improving · the offence scoring more · the scheme.* §4.21 measured this exact
decomposition: **team pass VOLUME is 7.5% of a receiver's year-over-year variance; his own SHARE is
93.4%.** And nobody forecasts team volume — ESPN's own correlation is **r=+0.231**, worse than using
last year's actual (+0.315).

**But the honest use is not zero.** A 7.5% lever cannot move a board and should never be an
argument for a player. **It is exactly the right size of thing to break a tie between two players
in the same range** — which is how Matt used it. Same standing as byes (§4.11) and the offensive
line (§4.24a): a tiebreak, never a number, never over real margin.

## 4. What I will not copy from the analysts

Matt: *"be able to support it like the analysts do it."* **The structure yes, the authority no.**
§4.13d measured the panel: the accuracy contest is **structurally blind to breakout skill** (being
10% tidier on ordinary players scores 2.80× as much as perfect foresight on every breakout); the
six-ranker panel is **one opinion measured six times** (mean pairwise r 0.81); and **disagreement,
controlling for ADP, predicts finishing WORSE** (−0.244, p=0.0009).

So: name the signals **in advance**, require a **count** rather than an argument, and report the
count. That is what an analyst's case looks like from the inside. Borrowing their conviction is
what §4.13d says does not work.

## 5. "Just because we can't measure it, we can't rule it out" — already the rule, and he is right

§4.24(b), in the directive since v6.4: ***"The RB version failed PREDICTION; this one failed POWER.
Keep the distinction if anyone revisits."*** Underpowered is not "no effect." Matt restated a rule
this project already holds, and applied it correctly.

The line that still holds: an unmeasured signal may be **carried**, **surfaced** and **used as a
tiebreak** — it may not be **scored into the board**. That is what §4.22 did with the `12g` badge
("surfaced, NOT scored") and it is the right template for everything in §3 above.

## 6. Matt's read of the project's evolution — agreed, and the record supports it

He asked whether the ladder, the cards and the badges were the beginning of a player-profile system
