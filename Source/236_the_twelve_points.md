## 2. THE HANDCUFF, PRICED CONDITIONALLY — THE FIRST TEST IN THIS PROJECT OF THE THING HE ACTUALLY MEANS

**THE TESTABLE FORM, stated before the run (§0.5(a2)):** *on a team whose lead back is absent in a
given week, what does the next man on that team score in that week, against what he scores when the
starter plays — and is he a startable RB?*

**POPULATION:** every team-season 2022–2025, weeks 5–14, where a lead back exists (the man with the
most touches in weeks 1–4, minimum 25 touches). **1,151 team-weeks, 181 with the lead back absent.**
**BASELINE — state it every time: half-PPR points under §2 from nflverse weekly; "startable" is at
or above doc 12's measured RB replacement of 9.92 ppg.** CI bootstrapped 4,000× **clustered by
team-season** (`ERROR_PATTERNS` A5).

| | n | ppg | touches | startable that week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **next man, lead back OUT** | 181 | **13.62** | 18.6 | **61.9%** |
| next man, lead back PLAYING | 970 | 6.32 | 8.8 | 22.8% |
| | | **+7.30** | | **CI [+6.01, +8.70]** |

**Frequency: 43.8% of lead backs miss at least one week 5–14; 1.41 weeks per team-season.**
**Tail: 18.2% of those weeks are 20+ points** — Pollard 33.2 behind Elliott, Sean Tucker 33.0 behind
Irving, **Rico Dowdle 31.9 and 30.9 behind Hubbard in 2025**, TreVeyon Henderson 29.8 behind
Stevenson. `[TESTED]`

**THIS DOES NOT CONTRADICT DOC 225, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS THE WHOLE POINT.** Doc 225 measured
**every** RB waiver add against **rest-of-season** ppg and found the uncontested RB the worst square
on its table (9.4% hit, 5.3% per attempt). **That is a different population and a different
outcome.** This measures the DIRECT next man on a team with a real lead back, scored **in the weeks
the starter is actually out.** Doc 225 kills "pick up running backs off the wire." It never tested
"roster the specific man who inherits a 300-point job." **Both stand. Quote the right one.**

**WHAT IT IS WORTH, AND THE PART THAT CORRECTS HIM.** His words: *"I'd much rather have another
teams backup RB since that is a double win… Jeanty in his current condition leaves me no choice but
to lose that option where I have a net gain at RB."*
- **His own handcuff is NOT a wash.** If Jeanty sits, the alternative to Washington is a wire back
  at **5.43 ppg** (doc 12) — not Jeanty. **13.6 against 5.4 is +8.2 a week**, and §4.19 says the
  wire cannot patch an RB hole on his own record (four of five never startable). The comparison he
  made was Washington against the man he lost; the right one is Washington against the man he could
  otherwise get.
- **A rival's handcuff pays less to his lineup and more to the standings.** In the weeks a rival's
  back is out, the inherited back displaces Matt's own RB3 (Dowdle, 10.85 a week) → **+2.8 a week**,
  or **+8 a week if one of his own backs is also out.** The **denial** half is real and is not
  measured anywhere: the rival gets 6.3 from whoever is left instead of 13.6, worth roughly
  **7 ppg × 1.4 weeks ≈ 10 points off that team's season** — but only decisive in the weeks Matt
  plays them. `[ARITHMETIC on the measured ppg, not a simulation]`
- **So both are live and neither dominates. Own cover protects a starting slot the wire cannot fill;
  a rival's handcuff adds a smaller lineup gain plus a denial that no test in this project prices.**
  His framing earns the denial term. It does not earn "my own handcuff is worthless."
