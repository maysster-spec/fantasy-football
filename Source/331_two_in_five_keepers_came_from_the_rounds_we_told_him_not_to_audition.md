## 3. RATE AND SHARE ARE DIFFERENT OBJECTS, AND SECTION 6 READS ONLY ONE

Section 4.18 measured the **rate**: of everyone drafted in rounds 5 to 8, 15.1% became a keeper;
of rounds 9 to 12, 8.3%. **True, and unchanged.** A round-5-to-8 pick is about twice as likely,
one for one.

This measures the **share**: of the keepers that actually happened, where did they come from.
**Nearly two in five came from round 9 or later**, because a 15-round draft has far more late
picks than early ones, and twice-as-likely on a much smaller base is still a minority of the total.

**Section 6 converted the rate into an instruction and dropped the share:**
> "Frequency halves and value goes to zero at the same seam, so a round-9+ pick is a 2026 dart,
> not an audition."

**That sentence is how a 39% source of keepers became a category he was told not to audition.**
The rate justifies *preferring* rounds 5 to 8. It does not justify *excluding* rounds 9 and later,
and the exclusion is what shipped.

## 5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**It establishes** that keepers in this league come from the late rounds often enough that
excluding those rounds from the audition is a real cost, and that section 6's instruction rests on
half the evidence.

**It does NOT establish that late keepers are WORTH as much.** Section 4.18b measured kept RBs
drafted round 9+ at **minus 2.5 VBD14 (n=7)**, and that number is untouched here. **Share is not
value.** This document counts keepers; it does not price them.

**So the two results are not in conflict, they are the two halves of the same question**, and the
missing half is exactly JOB 4: at equal price, is the ascending late player worth keeping? Until
that runs, the honest instruction is the weaker one:

> **Prefer rounds 5 to 8 for auditions, because the rate is twice as high. Do not EXCLUDE rounds
> 9 to 12, because two in five keepers came from there and three of Matt's own did.**

## 6. HIS STRUCTURAL POINT, WHICH IS THE REASON THE RULE IS SHAPED THIS WAY

His framing: by round 5 every roster's core is filled, so what is left on the board is by
construction the uncertain players, and the keeper rule pays out to whoever reads them correctly.
**Section 4.13b already measured the shape of that board without ever connecting it to keepers:**
startable rate is 57.7% at ADP 49 to 84, 37.7% at 85 to 120, **20.5% at 121 to 180.**

**So the eligible pool is, by design, the part of the board where four in five men fail.** Section
4.26(a) calls this the rule "fighting" the strategy and answers it by taking the most expensive
eligible player. **Matt's reading is that the uncertainty IS the game and the round-5 floor is what
makes it one.** Neither reading is measured. JOB 4 is written to decide between them, and this
document is why it is worth the run.
