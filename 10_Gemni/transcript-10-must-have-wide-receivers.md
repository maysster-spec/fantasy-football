### [01:15] 1. Nico Collins (Houston Texans)
**Ron Stewart:** ...with Nico Collins, who I don't want to say I wasn't down on. I will always be a Nico Collins believer. But the way we were heading into the season with Jaden Higgins, and potentially Jaden Higgins getting some of that fun off-the-line Z usage that we talked about when he was coming out of school—how these two mesh. If this maybe didn't change your rankings at all, but for me, I nudged him up a little bit even with Boutte coming in. **Matt Harmon:** Yeah, it probably just sort of solidified my confidence in Nico Collins as like a mid-second round pick. I have him right now as my 16th overall player; he goes 21st off the board on Yahoo drafts. And that's obviously ticked up ever since the Jaden Higgins injury. Higgins is such a bummer because he was one of these prospects that I had a mixed opinion about coming into the league—I wasn't sure if he could play as an X receiver in the NFL, and then he goes to Houston. It's like, well, he won't be a full-time X because he's playing with Nico Collins. He was getting rave reviews in camp, so that injury was a bummer.

When you sub out Higgins for Kayshon Boutte... I mean, my god, Ron, the ultimate sacrificial X receiver last year was Kayshon Boutte with the New England Patriots. The only guy who's been more of a sacrificial X that I've charted in the last three seasons is his backup, Kyle Williams. So he's not going to get that usage in Houston, which is interesting for Boutte, but it is a downgrade from Higgins. Honestly, I'm a big Jaylin Noel fan as well, a great late-round pick. But base case, subbing out Higgins for Boutte is just going to push more targets to Nico Collins.

When Nico's healthy—and that's been the biggest thing holding him back—he's been an elite receiver from a production standpoint. He's second since 2023 in yards per route run. From a Reception Perception standpoint, he is an elite press-man coverage beater. He's great on in-breaking routes over the middle of the field. CJ Stroud had a tough playoff finish, but he was a top-10 quarterback by EPA per play last year. In heavy personnel looks, Nico just dominates targets. Late second-round pick—I want to push that button every single time.

### [07:30] 2. Marvin Harrison Jr. & Michael Wilson (Arizona Cardinals)
**Ron Stewart:** Marvin Harrison Jr. has kind of been playing his NFL career so far on expert level difficulty. Kyler Murray can't see over the offensive line; Marv is playing strictly outside, pressed up against man. How are you treating the Cardinals receivers? How are you breaking ties, and can we see Marvin Harrison Jr. emerge as a fantasy contributor?

**Matt Harmon:** Look, I did a whole video on my channel with Jourdan Rodrigue where we spent an hour on why we are "Cardinals curious." This offense is going to be objectively so much better for both of these wide receivers than the previous unit. Michael Wilson noted that the reason he wasn't getting targets early last year was that he was playing the Z receiver in Drew Petzing's offense, where things flowed through the tight end (Trey McBride) and the X receiver (Harrison). It took an injury to Marv for them to move Wilson into the X.

In Mike LaFleur's offense, Wilson will play more Z closer to the core, involved as a blocker like Puka Nacua, while Marvin Harrison Jr. will be the outside X on the line of scrimmage. But this system uses tons of motion to blur those lines. Robert Mays noted that the Rams threw 133 passes to receivers in motion at the snap last year (most in the league); Arizona threw just 18. Arizona ran spread concepts 28% (third highest), while the Rams were at 7.3% (second lowest). Jacoby Brissett is functional, and LaFleur is a strong pass game designer.

Harrison fits this offense much better. People saying Marvin Harrison Jr. can't separate—get out of here! He's not the most explosive burner, but he's a souped-up craftsman, similar to Davante Adams. In Reception Perception, he clears 80th percentile vs. man and 77th vs. press. It's year three. Michael Wilson is also heavily discounted in drafts.

**Ron Stewart:** Mike Clay has Arizona first in pass attempts, and has Jeremiyah Love as RB8, with Tyler Allgeier backing up and Trey McBride as a top-two tight end. There is a lot of passing volume to go around.

### [15:40] 3. Rome Odunze (Chicago Bears)
**Ron Stewart:** Transitioning to another year-three guy: Rome Odunze. ADP has Luther Burden III and Colston Loveland ahead of him, but Caleb Williams could go nuclear. Where are you on Rome at this point?

**Matt Harmon:** I have Rome ranked ahead of Luther Burden this year, and I'm ahead of consensus ADP on both. The narrative that Ben Johnson drafted Burden and Loveland and "inherited" Odunze doesn't hold up—Ben Johnson inherited Odunze and ran the entire passing game through him over the first eight weeks. Odunze then tried to play through a foot fracture, which explains the split. In those first eight weeks, he was 19th in yards per route run.

Odunze is one of the best separators against press-man coverage in the NFL (93rd percentile success rate vs. press). He took 64.9% of snaps outside, 29% in the slot, and 6% in motion/backfield. He is a big target who details his routes well. Caleb Williams should take a step forward in year three. Just like Jaxon Smith-Njigba broke out in year three, Odunze has that exact ceiling.

### [22:00] 4. Zay Flowers (Baltimore Ravens)
**Ron Stewart:** What about Zay Flowers? Every time I draft him, people complain that he's too small to score touchdowns in a top implied-point offense. Is he misunderstood?

**Matt Harmon:** People remember Zay as a rookie when he was gadgety and had "brain farts" with drops or hopping backwards. But he eliminated that over the last year and a half. He is one of the premier zone-beating receivers in the league—90th percentile vs. zone as a rookie, 89th in year two, 91st last year. Last season, he jumped to a 75% success rate against press.

He follows the Amon-Ra St. Brown progression curve: elite vs. zone first, then improving vs. press-man to earn high red-zone targets. With Declan Doyle coordinating under the Ben Johnson tree, and with Rashod Bateman in a specialized role, Jacoby Lane as a contested-catch X, and an aging tight end room (Mark Andrews, Durham Smythe, Matthew Hibner), Flowers is set up for explosive plays and high targets. He has legitimate 8-touchdown and receiving-yard leader upside.

### [28:10] 5. Josh Downs (Indianapolis Colts)
**Ron Stewart:** Josh Downs has incredible per-route metrics, leading Indy's WR room in first downs per route run. But now Keenan Allen signs, and Downs picked up a training camp calf issue. How do you see the Colts' pass distribution shaking out?

**Matt Harmon:** Keenan Allen last year was essentially a designated hitter in 11 personnel (3+ WR sets). He played only 12 snaps in 21/22/12/13 personnel for the Chargers. Keenan led the NFL in third-down receptions, but he wasn't an every-down outside player.

Downs will play in heavier personnel packages and push vertically. In Reception Perception, Downs had an 82% success rate vs. press. Last year, Justin Herbert had no time behind the Chargers' line, forcing quick throws to Keenan over Ladd McConkey. The Colts' offensive line is far better. Downs just needs more routes and downfield targets (his ADOT was lower than Keenan's last year, which Shane Steichen must change). Daniel Jones executed Steichen's offense at a historic points-per-drive pace before injuries. Downs in the 80s/round 9 is a massive ceiling play.

### [35:20] 6. Jaden Reed & Packers Passing Attack (Green Bay Packers)
**Ron Stewart:** Speaking of per-route darlings, Jaden Reed. Green Bay has Josh Jacobs on the exempt list, MarShawn Lloyd stepping up, and they traded for Kaleb Johnson. Is it time to "let Jordan Love cook"?

**Matt Harmon:** Yes, absolutely. Josh Jacobs is on the exempt list and had endless red flags; I don't know if he plays this season. Green Bay is going to throw more, play more 11 personnel, and dramatically increase their pace. They were one of the slowest teams in the league, but they've been running uptempo and no-huddle in the preseason.

Reed will see the field plenty even if he's primarily in 3-WR sets. In my Reception Perception player comparison tool, Jaylin Noel and Reed share comps with Josh Downs and Curtis Samuel. Reed has 34 career carries for 310 rushing yards—he could see gadget backfield touches without Jacobs. Matthew Golden is fine at pick 124, and Christian Watson is the boundary WR1 coming off his ACL recovery, but Reed offers immense per-touch fantasy scoring.

### [45:00] 7. Jaylen Waddle (Denver Broncos)
**Ron Stewart:** Miami had quirky rotations, but now Jaylen Waddle is in Denver with Sean Payton after a trade involving a first-round pick. People seem squeamish about his fourth-round ADP. What's his outlook?

**Matt Harmon:** I am drafting Waddle every single time in the fourth round. Last year in Miami, he was 11th in yards per route run despite Miami ranking 32nd in plays run. Sean Payton confirmed Waddle will play the "movement Z" role—similar to Brandin Cooks in 2016. Courtland Sutton is the true X, and Pat Bryant is the power slot blocker.

From 2022 to 2024, Waddle played 77% outside and 81% on the line of scrimmage catering to Tyreek Hill's alignments. In Denver, he'll get free releases, motion layups, and deep play-action targets. Last year, Waddle averaged 4.17 yards per route run on play-action (7th in NFL), and Denver ran the 3rd-most play-action. He is in my tier 2 of wide receivers right alongside Chris Olave and DeVonta Smith.

### [52:15] 8. KC Concepcion (Cleveland Browns)
**Ron Stewart:** KC Concepcion is an electric rookie playmaker in Cleveland under Todd Monken. Is he just a gadget player, or a true technician?

**Matt Harmon:** I was shocked when charting him—he is the best pure separator in the 2026 rookie class. He posted a 92nd percentile success rate vs. man, 87th vs. press, and 81st vs. zone with an almost entirely green route tree. His only knock was catch-point technique; if he cleaned that up, he would have been my WR1 overall in this class.

He is not just a screen-and-sweep guy; he separates at all three levels. His rookie year should follow Zay Flowers' path: early designer touches expanding into a full-tree role. In the double-digit rounds, bet on his talent.

---

### [57:30] 9. Jalen Coker (Carolina Panthers)
**Ron Stewart:** Steve Smith put Jalen Coker on the map. With rookie Chris Brazzell out with a torn LCL, how does the Panthers receiver room look between Coker and Tetairoa McMillan?

**Matt Harmon:** The target distribution in Carolina is going to be ultra-concentrated between Tetairoa McMillan and Jalen Coker. Darren Waller is 33, and Tommy Tremble or Mitchell Evans won't command volume. In preseason, McMillan and Coker split inside/outside snaps 60/40. Playing Coker allows Carolina to move McMillan into the slot for 25–30% of snaps, similar to how Dave Canales utilized Mike Evans in Tampa Bay.

Coker is built like a young Chris Godwin—a physical power slot who can survive on the perimeter (79th percentile vs. press last year). Once he got healthy down the stretch in 2025, he produced big plays. He is an exceptional late-round redraft pick and dynasty target.
